# Wall Street cheers Huang's return; critics cry capture

*Week-one recap, Fri Nov 27, 2026, after the 1 PM ET half-day close. Source release: [NVIDIA, Nov 23, 2026](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-board-reinstates-jensen-huang-as-president-ceo-and-board-director).*

**Week one: NVDA ends up 19.9% but 8.8% below Monday's peak close, while Congress and the credit raters keep weighing in.**

NVIDIA's board unanimously reinstated Jensen Huang as President, CEO and director on Nov 23, citing input from senior levels of the US government. NVDA closed Monday at $165.05, up 31.5%; it fell 5.9% Tuesday and 3.9% Wednesday, then rose 0.8% to $150.49 in Friday's half-day session, ending the week up 19.9% but lagging a broad chip rally. S&P Global put NVIDIA's AA credit rating on watch for a downgrade, and Reuters, citing the FT, reported NVIDIA told partners to stop shipping servers to four Southeast Asian countries. In 457 verified quotes from 279 voices, opposition outnumbers support about two to one. No member of Congress on record supports the move. The administration doubled down: Trump said a pardon is "always an option," and Vice President JD Vance said "I don't think he needs a pardon at all." Huang faces a closed House Intelligence session on Dec 1 and a public Senate Banking hearing on Dec 3.

## What people said

**White House: all in.** Trump told CNBC "I believe my intervention was very important" and on Tuesday told the New York Post a pardon is "always an option, I guess," adding he found Huang "100% aboveboard." Attorney General Todd Blanche: "What does that mean had Huang been investigated? For what?" Spokesman Steven Cheung: "President Trump was totally involved and this is a great decision by the NVIDIA board." On Wednesday Vice President JD Vance told reporters "we were told that he was under no investigation," that "it was the board's decision to bring Jensen Huang back," and "I don't think he needs a pardon at all" (via CBS).

**Congress: bipartisan pushback.** Sen. Chris Van Hollen: "This is what a rigged system looks like." Sen. Bernie Sanders: "The fate of one man caused the stock market to lose and then regain trillions of dollars in value." Republican Sen. Rick Scott: "This looks terrible on its face." Sen. Katie Britt said Huang's actions "raised national security concerns." Sen. Kevin Cramer: "The worst part of this is what we may never know." On Wednesday Sen. Cory Booker, in a letter to board chair Harvey Jones, asked for records of the administration's talks with the board and whether NVIDIA is "too big to hold accountable" (Reuters). Sen. Kirsten Gillibrand: "A boardroom vote does not resolve a national security risk." California Attorney General Rob Bonta said it "does not bode well for enforcement of the law by the federal government without fear, without favor." Rep. Kathy Castor: "Reinstatement is no substitute for accountability."

**Governors.** Illinois Gov. JB Pritzker: "This is the greed and lack of accountability that the country just rejected." Pennsylvania Gov. Josh Shapiro called it "another example of the dangerous combination governing this country" (partial quote).

**Editorial pages and the right.** The FT's Lex column: "Jensen Huang's return is a triumph of money over morals." The WSJ editorial board called it "a political bailout": "That must be the quickest very thorough investigation in history." Bill O'Reilly: "I disagree with the president on the action." Laura Loomer: "Reinstating Jensen Huang is going to blow back on our national security." TheBlaze's John Tamny, for it: "Reason has prevailed, as has economic literacy with the reinstatement of Jensen Huang as CEO of Nvidia."

**Governance experts and investors.** Yale's Jeffrey Sonnenfeld: "we have seen no weaker board." NYC Comptroller Mark Levine: "They have proven they're not independent." Nada Kakabadse: "It hands the board's judgment to the state." Charles Elson: "It shows a weakness." Santa Clara University's Ann Skeet: "This could be a precursor to nationalization of companies like Nvidia." Thom Dennis: "A board that depends on political favor to operate can't claim to be exercising independent judgment." NYU's Scott Galloway: "Undisclosed affairs? As long as the stock’s up, it’s OK." American University's Kathleen Day: "The shareholders should demand a new board." Governance consultant Doug Chia (LA Times): "This is behaving like [Nvidia] is a state-owned entity."

**Activists and civil society.** Public Citizen's Robert Weissman: "Trump's tough talk on national security and all his anti-Chinese belligerence suddenly fades away when money—and Trump's personal investments—are at stake." National Women's Law Center's Fatima Goss Graves (AP): "If everybody shrugs their shoulders and says some people are too important to be held accountable, then we will have a problem." **Wall Street: upgrades, then caution.** Bernstein upgraded while calling the board's handling "frankly, clown-like"; Dan Ives raised his target to $250. By Tuesday Goldman's TMT desk wrote that "better sentiment is not the same as a clearing event for semis," and BofA: "Reinstatement should not be confused with the end of scrutiny."

**Abroad.** Israel's Benjamin Netanyahu: "Continuity in Nvidia's leadership is good news for that partnership and for the global AI industry." China's foreign ministry: "We have no comment on this." Singapore PM Lawrence Wong: "They have decided. So be it." China's Global Times: "Huang's reinstatement is unlikely to silence his opponents." In Taiwan, most people quoted on the street and online welcomed him back. "His contribution to Taiwan has been enormous," one told Reuters. The Korea Times editorial board: "Korea must prepare so as not to get cornered without plans B and C for its chip-dependent economy."

## Impact on the AI ecosystem

- The rally was broad across AI infrastructure customers and suppliers (neoclouds, memory, foundry, servers, optics), more than direct GPU rivals, which reads as relief for the whole capex chain.
- Asia followed on Tuesday: Taiwan's TAIEX closed up 9%, with TSMC limit-up; Korea's KOSPI rose 6.3% past 5,000 with SK Hynix and Samsung up about 6.7%; Japan's Nikkei rose 3.9%, led by Advantest (+12%) (Focus Taiwan, JoongAng Daily, Xinhua).
- The relief did not hold in New York: on Tuesday NVDA closed down 5.9% and the semiconductor index 7.9%, with Micron, Dell and CoreWeave down about 11-12% and AMD about 9%; Europe's chip stocks also fell (ASM International, Infineon). Reporting cited profit-taking, AI-spending doubts and the political overhang.
- On Wednesday the selloff spread to Asia and Europe: Taiwan's TAIEX fell 4.8%, Korea's KOSPI 6.2% (SK Hynix −11%, Samsung −10%), SoftBank 14.8%, ASML 5.8% and ASM International 7.8%. New York rebounded late: NVDA closed −3.9% after −9.1% at the low and the chip index only −0.7% after −8% (AP, Focus Taiwan, Yahoo, Motley Fool).
- By Friday chips rebounded: the chip index rose nearly 5%, Micron and Marvell about 7%, KOSPI 4.3% and SK Hynix 5.4%, while NVDA gained only 0.8%. Reuters, citing the FT, reported NVIDIA told partners including Dell and Supermicro to stop shipping servers to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia.
- Analysts frame the risk that just went away as leadership, not demand: Ives says "the demand side was never in question."
- Unresolved: export controls and China sales, the independent investigation, and Huang's Dec 1 closed-door interview with three House committees.

## Risks and open questions

- Investigation findings: the board's committee and counsel are still working; a negative finding would reverse today's premise.
- Export controls: Ives says "the export question has not been resolved"; Trump's own post asks NVIDIA to keep reviewing compliance.
- Governance credibility: the board reversed itself within days of saying it would not reinstate him; proxy advisers have not weighed in yet.
- Political exposure: Democrats promise investigations; Republicans Cotton, Cornyn, Cramer, Curtis, Ricketts, Bacon and the House China, Intelligence and Foreign Affairs chairs want answers; Huang testifies Dec 3.
- Credit: S&P Global put NVIDIA's AA rating on CreditWatch Negative on Nov 25, citing governance; a decision is due within 90 days.
